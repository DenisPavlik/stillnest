# The bake-off — not part of the site

`public/lab/` exists so one question can be answered with eyes instead of prose:
**does animating the interior photograph make it better, or worse?**

Open `http://localhost:3100/lab/hearth.html` after `pnpm build && PORT=3100 pnpm start`,
at 2560×1080 if you have it. Keys `0`–`3` switch modes; `H` hides the readout.

**Delete this whole directory before a release.** Nothing in `src/` imports it, and
`public/lab/bake.py` is the script that would move to `scripts/` if a direction wins.

## Round two

Round one offered four modes. The verdict on each is why this round looks the way it does:

| round one | verdict | what happened to it |
|---|---|---|
| breathing light alone | "I see no difference, just brighter" | no longer a mode; folded into the fire |
| flame warp, no embers | "the fire looks strange" | no longer a mode; the embers were what made it read |
| warp + embers | "much better" | **kept, unchanged** — it is mode 1 |
| snow beyond the glass | right idea, "badly made" | **rebuilt from scratch** — see below |
| the sound | "wind indoors" | **rebuilt** — nothing below 340 Hz any more |

## What each mode is

| key | layer stack |
|---|---|
| `0` | the approved photograph, alone — the control |
| `1` | the fire, drawn: warped flame, embers gated by the occluder matte, breathing light |
| `2` | beyond the glass: snow falling, the crowns moving, the room dead still |
| `3` | 1 + 2 |
| `4` | **the fire, generated**: a 490×410 patch from a fal.ai take, composited over the photograph |

Modes 1 and 4 are the real question: the same fire, drawn by a shader against generated by a
model, in the same frame at the same size.

Tuning: `C` composition clamp · `S` sound · `G` grain · `T` canopy on/off ·
`[` `]` ember count · `;` `'` ember glow · `9` `8` ember fan · `-` `=` warp ·
`,` `.` firelight · `o` `p` flake count · `n` `m` snow glow · `j` `k` sway.

## The one architectural claim, and how to check it

The photograph is a plain `<img>` and is never redrawn. Every moving layer is a canvas
whose backing store is the photograph's **native resolution for the rectangle it covers**,
scaled down by CSS — so the shaders sample 1:1 and the browser does the resample. Alpha is
the coverage, so each canvas is fully transparent everywhere it is not actually moving
something.

Measured at 2560×1080, mode 0 against mode 3, maximum deviation out of 255:

| region | maxΔ | share changed >2/255 |
|---|---|---|
| ceiling | **0** | 0.00 % |
| mezzanine railing | **0** | 0.00 % |
| table and chairs | **0** | 0.00 % |
| floor | 2 | 0.00 % |
| armchair | 1 | 0.00 % |
| timber at the right edge | 3 | 0.00 % |
| hood | 17 | 0.03 % |
| flue pipe | 12 | 0.43 % |
| deck either side of the fire | 19 | 0.13 % |
| the canopy — meant to move | 25 | 20.6 % |
| the fire — meant to move | 233 | 15.3 % |

**0.50 % of the frame changes.** The rejected `veo3.1/lite` clip changed **50.9 %**, its
bounding box was the whole frame, and its flue column peaked at **249/255**. Two orders of
magnitude, and the difference is not tuning — it is that nothing here repaints a pixel it
was not asked to move.

## Three things that had to be measured, not reasoned

1. **A canvas that covers a pixel it does not move still replaces it** — with the tile's
   pixel, and the tile is a separate JPEG pass. The canopy layer alone changed 2 % of the
   frame, including the deck and the hood, for no motion at all. Alpha is now tied to how
   much a pixel actually moves.
2. **The window mask cannot be a brightness key.** Cool-and-bright finds the sky and the
   lying snow and misses the trunks and the foliage in front of them, so 85 % of the snow
   died exactly where falling snow reads best — against something dark. The right test is
   `B − R`: the room is the only warm thing in this photograph, so one subtraction separates
   inside from outside and returns the mullions and the flue as crisp silhouettes for free.
3. **The mezzanine rail cannot be excluded by a height ramp.** The visible frame starts at
   photo v ≈ 0.37, so the rail *is* the top of it. It is carved out in the mask instead, and
   the small pocket of real glazing behind it is given up with it.

## Round three — fal.ai, and what it actually does

The owner asked for the generative route to be researched properly rather than dismissed on
one bad take. Two things were established, one by reading and one by spending.

**Masked video inpainting exists and is cheap.** `fal-ai/ltx-2.3-quality/inpaint` takes a
source video and a mask video, and its schema says in as many words: *"White regions are
regenerated; black regions are preserved from the source video."* The whole `ltx-2.3-quality`
family bills **$0.0024075 per megapixel** of generated video (width × height × frames), so:

| | pixels | cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1536×1024 × 121 frames | 190 MP | **$0.46** |
| 3072×2048 × 121 frames (the photograph's own size) | 762 MP | **$1.83** |
| 3840×2160 × 121 frames | 1004 MP | $2.42 |

`public/lab/falrun.py` implements that call end to end: it freezes the still into a video,
freezes the mask beside it, uploads both, submits, polls, and refuses to spend past
`--max-cost`.

**And then it was run, for $0.46, and both halves of the promise failed.**

1. *"Black regions are preserved"* is not literal. Measured against the frozen source, **64 %
   of the pixels outside the mask moved by more than 2/255, with a peak of 180**. Preserved
   means conditioned, not copied.
2. Worse: the fire it generated inside the mask was **poorer than the photograph's own**. The
   branching orange tongues collapsed into one pale narrow column and the logs lost their
   embers. The mask solved the geometry problem and cost us the thing we wanted.

**What worked instead.** The earlier `veo3.1/lite` take — already paid for — had a good fire;
every complaint about it was about the pipe and the sparks, never the flame. So take only its
fire: composite the clip through the same mask over the untouched photograph, crop the result
to the hearth, and ship that as a small patch.

Measured, mode 0 against mode 4, maximum deviation out of 255: **flue pipe 0, ceiling 0,
mezzanine 0, table and chairs 0, far window 0**, armchair 1, floor 1, deck 2, hood 6 — against
211 in the fire. **1.5 % of the frame changes and its bounding box is the hearth.** 490×410,
5 s seamless, 430 KB.

Outside the mask the composite fits the photograph at `1.0024 × plate − 0.12`, so there is no
colour-range shift between the JPEG and the H.264 — the thing that would otherwise make the
picture visibly change contrast the moment the loop faded in.

**The one honest caveat:** the model's flame is bigger and brighter than the one in the
approved photograph. The geometry is untouched; the composition is not.

## Sound

Synthesised in Web Audio, **zero bytes**, seamless because nothing repeats. Round one was
pink noise under a 620 Hz lowpass, which is a rumble — indoors that reads as wind, and the
whole proposition of this room is that the weather is on the other side of the glass. Now
every source passes a 340 Hz highpass, and what is left is a hearth from three metres: the
crackle of wood on a Poisson clock, and a thin dry hiss off the flame that rides the same
curve as the light.
