A quiet timber interior at dusk, seen from inside the room. A suspended steel fireplace burns in the middle of the floor; floor-to-ceiling glass along the far wall; snowy spruce forest outside.

Exactly four things move, and all of them move gently.
First, the fire in the round steel basin: the flames lean, rise and settle at a steady height, and the embers pulse deep orange along the logs.
Second, outside the house and beyond the glass, fine light snow drifts slowly down through the trees.
Third, outside the house and beyond the glass, the spruce branches and their needles sway very slightly in the cold air.
Fourth, the small recessed ceiling lamps hold a warm, slow, almost imperceptible breathing glow. They never blink and never flicker on and off.

Everything else is perfectly still. The camera does not move, zoom, pan or tilt. The steel flue pipe and the cone hood above the fire do not sway, bend or shift, and they keep their exact shape. The dining table and chairs, the armchairs, the timber walls, the floor, the mezzanine railing and the window frames are motionless. No snow, sparks, dust or floating particles anywhere inside the room. No people, no animals. The overall exposure, contrast and colour do not change from the first frame to the last.

Quiet, cinematic, low-light interior photography. Sharp, finely detailed, natural film grain.
